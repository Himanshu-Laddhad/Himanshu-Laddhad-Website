About me

I build data and AI systems that hold up in production: forecasting teams trust, pipelines that fail loudly instead of silently, and retrieval-grounded agents that turn weeks of manual work into hours.

I am a data scientist with three-plus years across enterprise analytics and AI engineering, currently completing an MS in Business Analytics and Information Management at Purdue University.

My work has followed one line: get the numbers right, then get them used. At JCB I rebuilt plant-level demand forecasting from spreadsheets into a validated Python pipeline, then moved to the business intelligence and quality side to own a curated analytics layer on SAP Datasphere, where the interesting problems were data contracts and typed exceptions rather than models. At Bosch I learned to rank exception categories by their actual contribution before touching anything. At PartnerLinQ I architected a retrieval-grounded mapping agent and trained a QLoRA-tuned small language model against it, measured honestly against the frontier-model pilot rather than claimed at parity.

The through-line is a preference for systems that survive contact with the people who depend on them. A model nobody trusts is a research artifact. A number nobody agrees on is a rumour. Most of my best work has been in validation, quarantine rules and the unglamorous plumbing that decides whether a forecast reaches a procurement desk at all.

I trained as a mechanical engineer before moving into data, which is probably why I still reach for the underlying mechanism before the model. Outside work I write on Medium about the parts of a build that usually get skipped.
