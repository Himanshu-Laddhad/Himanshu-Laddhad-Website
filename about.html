About me

I’m Himanshu, a data scientist who’s more interested in the “why” behind a number than the number itself.

I build things that sit between messy data and real decisions: models that forecast what’s coming, RAG systems that read through thousands of documents so someone doesn’t have to, pipelines that quietly catch a bad row before it turns into a wrong answer somebody trusted.

I did my undergrad in India, then came to the US for a master’s in business analytics at Purdue, and somewhere in between I fell into the habit of not fully believing a result until I’ve poked at it from three different angles. That habit has followed me through everything since, whether I’m fine-tuning a small language model, running a causal inference model to figure out what a promotion actually did versus what it looked like it did, or debugging why a pipeline is 15% off and nobody noticed for a week.

Lately I’ve been spending most of my time in the AI engineering side of things: building agents that retrieve and reason over real, messy, unglamorous data, comparing how a fine-tuned model holds up against a much bigger one, and figuring out where automation genuinely saves someone hours versus where it just moves the mess somewhere else. I like that this field rewards curiosity more than confidence. I’d rather be the person asking “wait, is this actually true” than the person with the fastest answer.

Outside of work, that same instinct just points at different things. I’m the person who wants to understand how something works before I trust it, whether that’s a model, a claim, or a decision someone made without checking their assumptions first.
